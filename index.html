    The trades behind every certificate

    A certificate of insurance (COI) is the one-page proof of insurance each subcontractor hands the contractor. Every one has an expiry date, and somebody has to notice when it runs out.

    How it would work

    Forward each certificate as it arrives.

    Once the pilot opens, your subs' certificates go to one inbox. Nothing new for your subs to learn.

    Then we log what it prints.

    Insured, insurers, policy numbers, dates and printed limits, exactly as printed. Anything unclear is held for a person.

    Then reminders go out in your wording.

    Before a printed expiry date, the sub is asked for an updated certificate, worded the way you approved.

    Download the free tracker

    Eight fictional sample certificates. Status compares the printed expiry date with today's date and says nothing about whether a policy meets any requirement. The free Excel tracker does the same for your own list, with no sign-up.

    We record. You decide.

    • No coverage advice

      We don't interpret policies, endorsements or contract terms, or tell you whether a policy is enough. A certificate is a summary; the policy itself governs.

    • No broker recommendations

      We don't recommend brokers, carriers or products, and we aren't paid by any of them.

    • No pass/fail judgements

      We show your entered requirements next to the printed values. Deciding what that means, and whether a sub can work, is up to you.
